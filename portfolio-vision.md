# Portfolio Website — Vision & Design Direction

A capture of the vision, taste, and design intent expressed across this chat.
This is the "why it should look and feel this way" document, separate from the
build specs.

---

## The core goal

A portfolio that gets me hired as a **product designer** in a hard NYC market,
on a 34-day OPT clock. It has to do two jobs at once:

1. **Pass the fast filter.** Recruiters spend 30–55 seconds on first pass. The
   site has to look consumer-grade and read as refined in seconds, or it gets
   closed.
2. **Prove I think.** My real edge isn't being the flashiest visual designer —
   it's research depth and design judgment. The portfolio should foreground
   *thinking and decisions*, with the visuals resolved enough to clear the craft
   bar.

The theme across my work is **designing for people and needs that existing
systems overlook or dismiss** (women's safety knowledge, PMOS management, large-
group booking, accessible creative therapy). Not "my own problems" — "the
overlooked," which I happen to be close to sometimes.

---

## What I want it to feel like

- **Refined, editorial, intentional.** Right now my pages feel like "AI slop" —
  a boring document. I want them to feel *placed* and considered, where every
  element belongs, the way Beverly's and Jackie's and Honey's do.
- **Confident, not timid.** Big, readable headlines. Left-aligned type that uses
  the page. Generous whitespace. Not small, thin, centered, cramped.
- **Calm pages, rich visuals.** The page itself is quiet (cream + ink). The
  color and life come from the product visuals sitting in their own blocks. That
  rhythm — calm surface, vivid product — is what stops it feeling flat.
- **Visuals that command their space.** Screens shown large and in device frames,
  each paired with a short headline and one line, like Jackie Nam's Dandi
  case study. Not thumbnails dropped onto the page.
- A little **whimsy and personality** later — a pet-able pixel-art / Kaisu-style
  character in a case study or on the about page. Playful, but secondary to the
  work.

## What I don't want

- Center-aligned everything (reads amateur).
- Thin, small, hard-to-read headlines.
- Low-contrast, dull, flat color.
- Clunky wide borders around images; images that look "dropped in."
- A portfolio that reads like a journal or a process dump. Editorial, not
  exhaustive. Save deep detail for the interview.
- Over-formatting, filler, or trying too hard. Plain and confident beats clever.

## Visual north star

- **Beverly Yip (bevyip.com)** — the primary north star for structure and
  overall feel. Achievable, clean, and it leaves room to add a Kaisu-style
  animated character later.
- **Jackie Nam (jackienam.com/dandi)** — the reference for *image treatment*:
  large feature visuals, each with a headline + one-line caption, screens shown
  big and in context, tidy rows of supporting images.
- **Honey Mehta** — admired for editorial polish, but judged too time-expensive
  to fully replicate right now. Borrow the spirit, not the months of refinement.

---

## The design system (the look)

**Type**
- Headlines: **Playfair Display** (500–600 weight — confident, not thin, not
  heavy). This fixes the "too thin / too small / hard to read" problem.
- Body: **Inter** (clean, readable).
- A clear hierarchy: big hero, strong section headlines, comfortable body,
  quiet captions.

**Color**
- Case study pages: **cream #FAF8F5** background, **ink #2A1F28** text. Neutral,
  high-contrast, calm. No plum, no product accents on the page itself.
- **Plum #7A3F5D** is my personal brand color — it lives on the homepage and
  About page, not inside case studies.
- Each product keeps its own palette *inside its visuals only* (Whspr amber,
  AIRA coral, Resy berry), so the pages stay a coherent family while each
  product still looks like itself.

**Layout**
- Everything left-aligned. Generous whitespace (80–120px between sections).
- Comfortable reading measure (65–70 characters).

**Images**
- Large, deliberately placed, in consistent device frames with soft shadows
  (no thick borders). Feature visuals big; supporting visuals in tidy rows.

## Structure & content philosophy

- **Two-layer reading:** scannable headlines + visuals + one-line takeaways on
  top (a recruiter gets the whole story in ~30 seconds), with prose underneath
  for whoever slows down.
- **Editorial, not journal.** Curate ruthlessly. Keep decisions and turns; cut
  documentation to a line. The question for every element: does this prove I
  think well?
- **Lead with the problem and the insight**, not process phases. The bootcamp
  "empathize → define → ideate" format is dead.
- **3 strong case studies** (2 short + 1 deep), not 10. Whspr is the hero.
- **Honest about scope and gaps** — name what's unfinished and what I'd do next.
  That reads as maturity, not incompleteness.
- **Surface "built with Claude Code"** where true (Whspr) — a real AI-native
  differentiator.
- **Keep it public**, no password gates.

## The homepage (front door)

- Honey/Beverly-style: left-aligned, a hero with rotating/layered photos, a
  2-up work grid, a Playground section, a footer ("Welcome to the wild side").
- Same visual system as the case studies (Playfair + Inter, the image
  treatment) so the whole site reads as one refined thing.
- Work cards link to the live case studies. The cards carry each product's
  identity (its color + a fine line-art hero drawing + the wordmark in Playfair).
- Hero line direction: "Product Designer / Creative Wildflower / Endlessly
  Curious."

---

## The site as a system (the four case studies as a set)

- **Consistent container, distinct contents.** Every case study page is the same
  neutral cream + ink + Playfair/Inter shell. Inside, each product's real
  palette shows in its framed visual blocks. This is what makes four different
  products read as one coherent portfolio instead of four different websites.
- Work-card hero illustrations: fine single-weight line drawings, one per
  project, each in the product's accent color, calm and editorial.

---

## Playground / personality (secondary, later)

- A Playground section for the more experimental/creative work (GestureSketch,
  Si.Ch, Nightmare in Neverland, etc.).
- Keep it clearly *secondary* to the case studies so I read as a product
  designer, not a creative-marketing generalist. (Hiring managers warned the
  playground/micro-interaction path can push you toward marketing creative work.)
- Phase-2 whimsy: a small pet-able pixel-art creature; marginalia (small honest
  margin asides in my voice next to images).

---

## The discipline (how I keep it on track)

- Applications stop the clock — not a perfect portfolio. Ship *passable*, apply,
  then keep improving while applications are already going out.
- Fight the perfectionism and the complet-every-process-doc instinct. Passable-
  and-shipped beats perfect-and-unpublished.
- Build in focused sequential sessions, one concern at a time. Don't let a new
  shiny project (or endless polish) delay applying.
